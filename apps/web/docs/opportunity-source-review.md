# Opportunity: source review for the reading room

The five chapters use the same four story beats in comic, prose and scroll editions. Narrative voices and diagrams are imagined and labelled. The completed books published by this web update are Eagle and Opportunity; the old other-machine draft is not imported.

## Verified sources

- [NSSDCA registry: 2003-032A](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=2003-032A): designation, architecture and launch time.
- [JPL airbag engineering](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/how-to-land-softly-on-a-hard-planet/): four interconnected airbags, each with six lobes; Vectran material.
- [JPL Berry Bowl investigation](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/mineral-in-mars-berries-adds-to-water-story/): comparing clustered spheres with berry-free rock; hematite result and geological interpretation.
- [JPL Purgatory image](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia07999-looking-back-at-purgatory-dune/): Sol 446 trapping; approximate ripple size; more than five weeks of recovery; the archived image was acquired after escape.
- [JPL Education engineering account](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/teachable-moment/mars-rover-mission-comes-to-an-end-but-science-legacy-live-on/): Earth test rover and recovery planning.
- [JPL marathon report](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-opportunity-mars-rover-passes-marathon-distance/): 24 March 2015; 42.195 km cumulative milestone; 46.5 m milestone drive.
- [NASA Science mission overview](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/): rover mass, instruments, cleaning events, memory workarounds and mission history.
- [JPL storm updates](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/opportunity-hunkers-down-during-dust-storm/) and [recovery account](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/six-things-about-opportunitys-recovery-efforts/): final contact, tau and inferred—not observed—fault states.
- [NASA last-images record](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/last-images-opportunity-took/): PIA22930 thumbnails; distinct from last transmitted incomplete frame PIA22929.
- [JPL mission-end report](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-opportunity-rover-mission-on-mars-comes-to-end/): original 90-Martian-day / 1,000 m design goal, more than 1,000 recovery commands, final attempt and completion announcement.
- [JPL 2026 record report](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-sets-off-world-record-for-total-distance-driven/): Perseverance reached 45.22 km on 11 October 2026, exceeding Opportunity’s 45.16 km. This appears as a later legacy outcome, never a new Opportunity transmission.

## Corrections to the supplied draft

| Draft claim | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 24 separate bulletproof airbags | Four airbags / 24 lobes. Do not call the spacecraft fabric bulletproof. |
| A 90-sol warranty or certain failure date | A primary-mission design goal, not a warranty. |
| Hematite only forms in water; pure hematite berries | JPL identifies the major iron-bearing mineral and combines multiple lines of evidence for the concretion interpretation. |
| Identical 2 cm daily progress; 13 years always driving backwards | Omitted: the cited sources do not support those universal claims. |
| Flash Bank 3 failed | Omitted. The current NASA mission history identifies Bank 7; the chapter discusses memory workarounds without asserting one instantaneous failure mechanism. |
| Gypsum proves safe drinking water | Do not equate mineral evidence with verified human water safety. |
| Tau 10.8 means 99.998% of all sunlight is blocked | The interactive calculation is explicitly a simplified direct-beam exp(−tau) model, excluding scattered light and solar-array/battery predictions. |
| The final packet proved clock and heater failure | Engineers inferred possible fault states after contact ended. |
| PIA22521 is a final camera frame | It is a simulated darkness graphic. Use the separate final-image archive records with their correct captions. |
| 217,594 images all came from Pancam | Not attributed to Pancam; the rover carried several cameras. |
| All artifacts’ exact positions and elevations are already verified | The precision table is not published. A GIS entry requires a cited observation, frame and uncertainty; the supplied table conflicts with existing values. |
| Mass becomes 69.9 kg on Mars | Mass remains the same; gravitational weight is a force. The reading room uses the sourced surface-rover mass. |

The supplied direct image/model/video links are not assumed valid by ID pattern. The reading room uses image URLs extracted from their actual NASA/JPL archive pages, with source links and viewpoint descriptions.
